Artem’s story

Creating my own table

For a long time, my career followed a path that made perfect sense.

I worked in professional kitchens, grew as a chef, developed my pastry skills, created menus, built kitchen systems, and helped open one café project after another.

I loved creating something from nothing. I loved watching an idea become a working kitchen, a menu, a team, and eventually a real business.

But at some point, I had a simple realization:

I had become very good at helping other people build their projects while continuing to postpone my own.

This year, something in my mindset changed. I decided to stop waiting for the perfect moment and take responsibility for what came next.

That decision led me into a completely different chapter of my career: private dining.

I began working as a private chef for affluent families, and with one family in particular, I stayed for an extended period of time. That experience became one of the most valuable schools of my career.

A restaurant works differently. You create a menu. You refine the dishes. Guests arrive and choose from what you have already designed.

A private chef does not have that luxury.

When you cook for the same person again and again, simply preparing their favorite meal well is not enough.

You have to make them curious about tomorrow.
And then again next week.
And again next month.

I constantly had to search for new ideas, ingredients, combinations, and presentations. I revisited familiar dishes and looked for ways to see them differently. I combined classical techniques with modern ideas. I took something simple and searched for the one detail that could make it feel new again.

Sometimes the most difficult part was not cooking at all. It was understanding the person I was cooking for.

What do they feel like eating today?
What have they had recently?
What worked last time?
How can I surprise them without turning dinner into an experiment simply for the sake of being different?

That experience taught me something that has now become one of the principles behind ELART:

A great menu does not begin with a dish. It begins with the person you are creating it for.

That period pushed me to become a much more creative chef. I stopped seeing a recipe as the final destination. It became a starting point.

Tradition can meet something new.
A classic can evolve without losing its identity.
And sometimes one thoughtful detail can turn a simple dish into something people remember.

I am deeply grateful for that chapter because it helped me understand the kind of chef I want to be. Not simply someone who can execute another person’s concept well — but someone capable of creating his own.

That is where ELART truly began for me.

I want to bring everything I have learned throughout my career into this company: the discipline of restaurant kitchens, pastry, menu development, kitchen systems — and, most importantly, the personal approach that private dining taught me.

Together with Olena, we are not building ELART as a company with an enormous catalog of predetermined dishes. Of course, we have menus and combinations we know and love. But for us, every meaningful event begins with a different question:

Who are we cooking for — and how do they want to remember this evening?

A wedding, a family celebration, or an important dinner may be another event on a caterer’s calendar. But for the person who trusted us with it, that day may happen only once.

My responsibility as a chef is to make sure the food becomes part of the reason they remember it. Not simply to serve dinner — but to create something that feels like it was made for you.
